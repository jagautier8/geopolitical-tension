US prepping Gulf allies for renewed Iran strikes after Nov 3 midterms

The catalyst: CNBC reports the US has told Gulf countries to prepare for a resumption of strikes on Iran after the midterm elections — this is the headline that broke WTI through the $103.92 wall that had capped every bounce since the war began. Confirmed Wed Oct 28 close: WTI settled $102.78 (+2.9%), Brent $115.32 (+1.3%); live quote now $104.40 (+2.35%, day high $104.99) is running above that settle into Thursday. Compounding factors: (1) a senior US military officer briefed Trump at the White House Oct 29 on Iran military options (WSJ); (2) Trump has repeatedly signaled strikes resume after Nov 3; (3) parallel diplomacy track is NOT dead — VP Vance sent a letter to Iran security chief Rezaei to revive talks, and Qatar handed Tehran a new draft deal, but a senior Iranian official rejected it as "unacceptable ideas," not a real offer (Reuters). Iran's FM still claims diplomacy has a chance and floats a 7-day plan. Net effect: strike-prep fear is winning over diplomacy hope right now — that tension (not a clean escalation) is exactly why the breakout is real but fragile: UBS's Staunovo flagged oil "under pressure amid renewed diplomatic efforts" the very next session, meaning this can reverse as fast as it broke out if Vance's outreach or Qatar's draft gets any traction.
